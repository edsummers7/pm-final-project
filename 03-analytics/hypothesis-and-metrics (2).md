## Read your data snapshots
- **Does the funnel data confirm your M2 friction point, or does it tell a different story? Note where the numbers align with the qualitative pain you found and where they diverge.:** Where the data aligns with my M2 friction point

Findability is getting worse. Content searched then played fell from 41% to 34% (down 7 pts), so fewer searches end in someone watching. This matches the qualitative pain that search returns and discovery fail users in a large library.
The funnel leaks at the discovery stages. Of the visitors who browse (71%), about a third never open a title page (48%). Of those who reach a title page, about 40% never press play (29%). That fits the "overwhelmed by choice, can't find the one thing" pain.
Engagement is falling overall. Daily Active Users are down 21%, and average session length is down 23% (31 to 24 minutes). Users who can't find something they want are less likely to stay or return.

Where it diverges or adds nuance

The top of the funnel is holding. People are getting in and browsing, so the problem isn't acquisition. My M2 friction was framed as a search and navigation problem, but the largest leak is later in the funnel.
The biggest drop is after play starts. Only 38% of people who start playing reach a 30+ minute session (29% down to 11%), and sessions reaching 30+ minutes fell from 19% to 11%. Findability explains why people fail to start something, but not why those who do start don't stay. Relevance or content quality is probably a factor too.
Spotlight reach is only 18%, so most users haven't experienced any curation yet.

Conclusion: The data confirms the friction point but doesn't change my target. The cluttered library is still a real driver, because failed discovery shows up in search-to-play and in the browse-to-play steps. But I should reframe the problem around depth of engagement, which means helping users find something worth watching, not just something findable.

## Step 3 · Craft your hypothesis
- **Qualitative evidence (from M2) , quote the specific friction / moment of misery for your persona:** Users with a descriptive or situational intent ("something worth watching tonight") can't express it, so they browse long sessions that end without a play. Research participants described the library as a "warehouse", wanted to be told "what's good tonight", and in some cases churned to a competitor's hand-picked emails.
- **Quantitative evidence (from M3) , name the metric or data point that confirms the pain; cite the number:** Content searched then played fell from 41% to 34%.
Month 0 to Month 1 is the biggest leak in every cohort (Full Library cohorts retain 63 to 68% at Month 1).
Spotlight cohorts retain 74 to 79% at Month 1, and Spotlight cuts Wanderer churn by 22 points.
Only 11% of visitors reach a 30+ minute session, down from 19%.
- **Persona , role, goal, and the friction you confirmed in the reconciliation steps:** A Wanderer (41% of the base, $8.40 LTV, 1.1 sessions a week, 61% trending content). Their goal is to find something worth watching without effort. They're blocked by a cluttered library, so they default to the trending row, abandon the session, and look at competing services.
- **Problem you are solving , one sentence describing the specific friction this initiative removes:** Wanderers can't find something worth watching in a cluttered library, so they leave in their first month before a viewing habit forms.
- **Strategic outcome , what behaviour change do you expect, and how does it map to retention / revenue / churn?:** Spotlight surfaces curated, relevant titles in early sessions. I expect Wanderers to move from browsing without playing to starting and finishing titles, which should lift Month 1 retention, reduce churn and move their LTV toward Casual Browser levels ($11.40).
- **Primary success metric (initiative signal) , the leading indicator that tells you the gap is closing:** Month 1 retention of Spotlight-exposed Wanderers, compared with a non-exposed holdout. Proposed target: +10 points. The Full Library Nov cohort is at 63%, and the Spotlight cohorts reached 74 to 79%.
- **Guardrail metric (product signal) , the metric that must NOT drop; it protects your existing base:** Power User sessions per week (currently 4.8) and their low churn. The 22% of users with the highest LTV must not be pushed into a curated experience that makes their library harder to use. As a second guardrail, average session length must not fall below 24 minutes.
- **Decision window , how much time or data before you scale, pivot, or kill? minimum threshold to proceed?:** Eight weeks, long enough to read Month 1 retention for two full cohorts. Scale if the exposed group beats the holdout by at least 8 points with guardrails intact, iterate if the gain is 3 to 8 points, and kill if it's under 3 or a guardrail drops.
- **Draft your full hypothesis sentence , one to three sentences; quote the metric, name the persona, name the outcome:** Based on research showing that Wanderers can't find something worth watching in a cluttered library, and on data showing that Month 1 is the biggest leak (63 to 68% retention for Full Library cohorts, against 74 to 79% for Spotlight cohorts), I believe that solving discovery with Spotlight for Wanderers will result in more of them forming a viewing habit in their first month, as measured by a 10-point increase in Month 1 retention. I will protect Power User sessions per week and average session length, and will make a go/no-go decision after eight weeks.
